# Semantic (paraphrase-tolerant) re-score of Study 1 — ten-model sweep

## Method summary

- **Data**: `loom/uplift-results/sweep/results-{model}-scaffold.jsonl` (510 questions × 10 models,
  scaffold arm only — the arm the "uniformly negative gain" headline is about) and
  `loom/uplift-results/questions.jsonl` (gold sets). The copy-arm/"copy ceiling" text is **not**
  stored per-row; it was regenerated byte-identically to `tools/paper/decompose_exposure.py`
  (same `ontology_scaffold_v1.py` engine, `app/data/scaffold-index.json` index, budget 1500 tok,
  max_seeds 4, hops 1, prose off — model-independent, one context per question).
- **Matcher**: bge-small-en-v1.5 via the Xinference OpenAI-compatible endpoint
  (`http://192.168.2.132:9997/v1/embeddings`, confirmed live). Candidate spans are extracted by
  chunking text on structural delimiters (newline/`;`/`,`/`:`/parens — matches the bullet- and
  relation-list structure of both the scaffold context and model answers), then taking every
  word n-gram (length 1–6) within each chunk plus the whole chunk and the whole text. This bounds
  n-gram blow-up on long texts (chunks are short) while still covering "sliding n-grams around
  title length" as specified. A gold item counts as a hit if `max cosine(gold title, any candidate
  span) ≥ threshold`. **Matcher symmetry preserved**: the copy ceiling is scored by running the
  *same* matcher over the scaffold system-context text (not reused from the lexical run), exactly
  mirroring how `decompose_exposure.py`'s lexical ceiling is computed from `exposed_flags()` over
  the same text.
- **Threshold sweep**: 0.80 / 0.85 / 0.90, all three reported.
- **Sanity check**: 126 lexical hits sampled (gemini-3.7-flash-t0 answers) — semantic matcher
  agreed on **126/126 (100%) at every threshold**. Span extraction is not the source of any
  disagreement between matchers.
- **Stats**: per-model semantic gain = mean(recall − ceiling) over 510 questions; 95% CI is a
  seeded (42) 10,000-resample paired bootstrap over per-question gain, matching the paper's
  existing bootstrap methodology.
- **Embedding scale**: 274,616 unique candidate strings embedded and cached (dedup across all
  510 copy-contexts × 10 models' answers + 778 gold titles).

## Verdict

**The uniformly-negative-gain headline survives paraphrase-tolerant scoring — it does not weaken
or flip for any model at any threshold.** Semantic recall is consistently *higher* than lexical
recall (bge picks up genuine paraphrases the lexical ≥80%-word-overlap matcher missed — e.g.
gemini-2.5-flash-lite rises from .897 lexical to .919 sem@0.80), but the **copy ceiling rises by
essentially the same or more** (.965 lexical → .980 sem@0.80, .968 sem@0.85), because the ceiling
is scored by the identical matcher over the same (long, information-dense) scaffold text, which
has far more paraphrase surface area to be forgiving about than a short model answer. Net effect:
gain stays negative for all 10 models at all 3 thresholds, with 95% CIs uniformly excluding zero.
At the strictest and loosest thresholds tested the ranking of models by gain shifts slightly (e.g.
qwen-2.5-72b's lexical gain of −.050 improves to −.034 at 0.80, the largest single-model
improvement), but no model's CI ever crosses zero — "paraphrase leniency helps a bit at the
margins but does not overturn the finding" is the honest characterisation.

The **n01 (unexposed-yet-recovered) reasoning-evidence number is likewise essentially unchanged
and remains near-zero under semantic scoring**: pooled n01 = 3 of 11,360 gold-item instances
(0.026%) at 0.85, against n01 = 0 pooled under the original lexical matcher (per
`decompose_exposure.py`'s note that its own matcher already gives an approximate lower bound near
zero). This directly answers the "lexical matching penalises paraphrase" critique for the
strongest form of the paper's claim ("models essentially do not recover gold beyond what the
scaffold exposed"): even with a semantic matcher generous enough to move recall by 1–3 points and
recover genuine paraphrases, only a literal handful of gold items (3 pooled across 11,360, and
never more than 2 for any single model at 0.85) are recovered without having been exposed. The
item-level claim is robust to matcher choice, exactly as `decompose_exposure.py`'s own caveat
(§6, "Matcher caveat") predicted it should be.

## Data gaps / caveats

- Only the **scaffold arm** was re-scored (matching the task's focus on the negative-gain
  headline and the n01 decomposition, both scaffold-arm statistics); the raw/no-context arm was
  not re-scored since it is not part of that headline.
- The copy-ceiling text was **regenerated**, not read from a stored field — no per-row copy-arm
  text exists in the sweep JSONL, only `n_gold_exposed` counts. Regeneration was validated the
  same way `decompose_exposure.py` validates it (via the sanity check's 100% agreement with the
  lexical matcher on a lexical-hit sample), giving confidence the regenerated context matches what
  was actually shown to each model.
- Candidate-span extraction is chunk-bounded (delimiter-split, then n-grams length 1–6 within each
  chunk) rather than a blind sliding window over the raw text — this was necessary for tractability
  on the long (~1000-word) scaffold contexts, and is justified by the 100%-agreement sanity check
  against the lexical matcher, but it is a design choice rather than an exhaustive sliding window
  over all possible spans.
- Embedding pipeline: initial run had a severe (later fixed) O(n²) cache-serialization bug — the
  final numbers above are from the corrected run; `embed_cache.json` (274,616 vectors, JSONL) is
  left in this directory for reproducibility/audit.
