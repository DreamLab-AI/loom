When a language model answers from a curated corpus via graph-based retrieval, reported grounding uplift mixes delivery of facts already shown with reasoning over injected structure. We propose exposure accounting: classify every gold item by whether the context exposed it and whether the answer recovered it, and report the four counts. The copy ceiling, the recall a verbatim copy of the context achieves, is that table's scalar and the signed gain over copy its net, both judge-free. Across ten models, unaided recall averages 0.26 and grounded recall 0.92, yet gain over copy is uniformly negative (-0.067 to -0.022). Of 11,360 gold-item observations (1,136 target instances under ten models, not 11,360 independent facts), three unexposed items are recovered. A stratified audit of 423 of those observations, under a cross-family judge and a symmetric quote gate, puts the precision of the matcher's credit at 0.971 [0.941,0.986]. On 60 unexposed uncredited items the judge finds no hidden correct answer (upper bound 6%); all three unexposed credited items, a census, fail adjudication, so credit beyond exposure is rare and here spurious. Correcting the exposed-item rate for judged relational correctness gives an estimated 0.905 against 0.931 under this judge. On gold targets the scaffold did not expose, recovery falls from 0.121 bare to 0.004 grounded (0.093 to 0.000 relationally judged): injection suppresses parametric recall the bare model has. Rephrasing out of the graph's title vocabulary collapses exposure from 0.964 to 0.328, while the absence-keyed fallback fires on 2 of 506. A paired production study lifts judged quality by +0.27 pooled. Negative controls show the served path beating no context, but not that the scaffold's content matters beyond a well-formed on-corpus block. Evaluations whose gold derives from the injected corpus should report the accounting.
