When a language model answers from a curated corpus via graph-based retrieval, reported grounding uplift mixes delivery of facts already shown with reasoning over injected structure. We propose exposure accounting: classify every gold item by whether the shown context exposed it and whether the answer recovered it, and report the four counts. The copy ceiling, the recall a verbatim copy of the shown context would achieve, is that table's scalar and the signed gain over copy its net, both deterministic and judge-free. Across ten models, unaided recall averages 0.26 and grounded recall 0.92, yet gain over copy is uniformly negative (-0.067 to -0.022). Of 11,360 gold-item observations (1,136 target instances under each of ten models, not 11,360 independent facts), three unexposed items are recovered. A stratified audit of 423 of those observations, adjudicated by a cross-family model judge under a verbatim-quote gate, puts the precision of the matcher's credit at 0.971 [0.941,0.986] and false credit on unexposed items at 0 of 60; correcting the exposed-item rate for judged relational correctness moves it from 0.931 to 0.905. On this corpus's gold targets that the retrieved scaffold did not expose, recovery falls from 0.121 bare to 0.004 grounded: injection suppresses parametric recall the bare model has. Rephrasing questions out of the graph's title vocabulary collapses exposure from 0.964 to 0.328, while the absence-keyed fallback fires on 2 of 506 questions. A paired production study lifts judged quality by +0.27 pooled. Negative controls show the served path beating no context, and do not establish that the scaffold's specific content matters beyond a well-formed on-corpus block. Evaluations whose gold derives from the injected corpus should report the accounting.
