When a language model answers from a curated corpus via graph-based retrieval, reported grounding uplift mixes delivery of facts already shown with reasoning over injected structure. We propose exposure accounting: classify every gold item by whether the shown context exposed it and whether the answer recovered it, and report the four counts. The copy ceiling, the recall a verbatim copy of the shown context would achieve, is that table's scalar and the signed gain over copy its net, both deterministic and judge-free. Across ten models, unaided recall averages 0.26 and grounded recall 0.92, yet gain over copy is uniformly negative (-0.067 to -0.022). Of 11,360 gold-item observations (1,136 target instances under each of ten models, not 11,360 independent facts), three unexposed items are recovered. Such a score does not establish reasoning beyond answer-name exposure; it bounds what an evaluation can attribute. On this corpus's gold targets that the retrieved scaffold did not expose, recovery falls from 0.121 bare to 0.004 grounded under this authority instruction and retrieval policy: injection suppresses parametric recall the bare model has. Rephrasing questions out of the graph's vocabulary collapses exposure from 0.96 to 0.34, while the absence-keyed fallback fires on 3 of 510 questions. A paired production study lifts judged quality by +0.27 pooled (exact signed-rank p=0.0023, a rank estimand, not a test of the mean). A control rerun under a common retry policy shows the scaffold beats no context, but withholds the claim that the retrieved content beats length-matched on-corpus text; that rerun's rows are reported here but not yet released. A two-edge composition test shows route information changing answers whose names were already exposed: exposure and reasoning need separate instruments. Evaluations whose gold derives from the injected corpus should report the accounting.
