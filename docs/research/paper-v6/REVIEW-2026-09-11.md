## 1. Resolve before scientific sign-off

### 1.1 The claimed verbatim-copy score is not implemented symmetrically

**Confirmed code/definition mismatch; impact on the frozen questions needs data.**

Locations: `main.tex:80`–105 and 128–135; `tools/paper/decompose_exposure.py:109`–136 and 203–264.

The paper defines both answer recall and copy recall as the fraction of gold targets matched. The released implementation instead does the following:

- `score_answer_recall()` returns **1 if any target is hit** for a question with `gold_type == "any"` (documented in the code as T-COMMON).
- The copy ceiling is always `sum(exposed_flags) / len(gold)`, including those same questions.
- Therefore, using the same target matcher does **not** guarantee that returning the input verbatim gets the reported ceiling under the headline answer scorer.

Counterexample: two alternative valid gold targets, only one present in context. The implemented ceiling is 0.5; returning that context verbatim scores 1 under the answer scorer. The supposed no-op can have positive "gain over copy" without recovering anything beyond exposure.

The later semantic scorer (`uplift-results/semantic-rescore/rescore.py`, Phase D) applies the `any` collapse to **both** answer and ceiling, so its aggregation also differs from the lexical implementation. This is more than changing the matching function.

**Required resolution:** establish whether the affected frozen questions ever have multiple alternatives/partial exposure. Define one explicit question-scoring function and apply it to both the model answer and the exact copy baseline. Recompute affected tables, plots and intervals, or prove from frozen rows that this mismatch is numerically inert. Keep item-level decomposition separate from question-level alternative-answer scoring. Add an executable invariant: scoring the chosen copy text as an answer must exactly equal its reported copy baseline for every question type.

### 1.2 The exposure text in code includes the prompt, not just the scaffold

**Confirmed code/definition mismatch; numerical impact unverified.**

Locations: `main.tex:80`–94; `decompose_exposure.py:129`–136 and 214–221.

`exposed_flags(messages, gold)` concatenates **every string message**, including the user's question. The paper defines `S_i` as the exact scaffold and describes a no-op returning that scaffold. These are different inputs. Prompt-visible gold could count as exposed even if the injected block omits it; it also undermines the statement that raw exposure is approximately zero merely because no scaffold is supplied.

**Required resolution:** choose and document scaffold-only or full-visible-input exposure, align the implementation, and test prompt-only hits explicitly. State the raw-arm convention consistently. If the scaffold-only definition is retained and the matcher rejects an empty string, its raw ceiling is exactly zero, not approximately zero.

### 1.3 The semantic robustness paragraph contradicts its saved summary

**Confirmed reporting mismatch, with additional inconsistencies in the summary itself.**

Locations: `main.tex:125`–126; `uplift-results/semantic-rescore/RESCORE.md` and `rescore.py`.

The manuscript says semantic scoring raises every model's recall, raises the ceiling as much or more, and leaves beyond-exposure recovery unchanged at 3/11,360 across the threshold sweep. The saved summary does not support that wording:

- At threshold 0.90, Gemini 3.7 Flash recall is **0.920**, below lexical **0.942**, and its ceiling is **0.937**, below the lexical value. The other models' 0.90 recalls are also below their lexical values.
- At 0.80, several models improve their gain. For example, Qwen-2.5-72B changes from approximately **−0.050 to −0.034**. Thus the ceiling does not rise "as much or more" for every model.
- The summary establishes pooled `n01 = 3` specifically at **0.85**. Elsewhere it says a single model reaches `n01 = 4` at 0.90, which cannot coexist with a pooled count of 3 at that threshold.
- The summary calls 0.90 "more permissive", although a higher cosine threshold is stricter.
- It incorrectly calls the original lexical pooled `n01` zero in one paragraph, while the saved decomposition gives 3.
- Its displayed lexical ceiling of 0.965 is inconsistent with the saved decomposition's **0.964477**, which rounds to 0.964 at three decimals.
- The summary describes agreement on a sample of answer hits as validation of byte-identical context regeneration. That check cannot establish byte identity, or even per-question context-exposure identity.

The potentially defensible result is narrower: the saved summary reports negative semantic gains with individual bootstrap intervals excluding zero for all 30 model/threshold combinations. That is encouraging, but cannot certify the contradictory recovery counts or fix issues 1.1–1.2.

**Required resolution:** regenerate a machine-readable threshold table and all four decomposition counts at each threshold. Describe the embedding model (`bge-small-en-v1.5`), span extraction, thresholds, aggregation and bootstrap in the paper. Supply the missing `embed_lib.py`, contexts/cache or equivalent reproducible assets; the released `rescore.py` currently cannot run from this checkout. Distinguish model-free lexical scoring from embedding-model-dependent scoring.

### 1.4 The zero-attrition control contrasts do not add up on a common sample

**Confirmed arithmetic inconsistency if the advertised ITT rows share the same questions; differing subsets could explain it but are not reported.**

Locations: `main.tex:364`–398, Table `tab:controls`.

For a fixed common set of paired questions, mean contrasts must be additive. The new/ITT column gives:

| Implied from arm-minus-raw means | Arithmetic | Reported direct contrast |
|---|---:|---:|
| true − irrelevant | 0.75 − 0.44 = **0.31** | **0.28** |
| true − shuffled | 0.75 − 0.94 = **−0.19** | **−0.23** |
| true − masked | 0.75 − 0.62 = **0.13** | **0.08** |
| true − fluent-noise | 0.75 − 0.46 = **0.29** | **0.25** |

These discrepancies exceed ordinary two-decimal rounding. They can result from different paired subsets, different retained raw rows, weighting, or inconsistent extraction of summaries. The table gives no per-contrast `n`, and the manuscript alternates between "0% attrition", `n=54`, and approximately 50–55 rows per arm. The original cohort was 57 in-domain questions, one of which had no scaffold, leaving 56 engaged control questions.

**Required resolution:** publish per-arm counts, per-contrast counts and question IDs, missingness/skip reasons, raw-arm budget provenance and exact unadjusted/adjusted p-values. Recompute a common-cohort table or explicitly label pairwise-complete subsets. A rerun at 4096 with selective 8192 retries should be called a common **retry policy**, not a literally uniform final token budget, unless pairs were synchronised. Explain how non-engaged questions enter an "intention-to-treat" analysis.

The new control rows and Gemini rejudgements are not present among the reviewed `paper-v2` artifacts; the existing `analyze_controls.py` computes the historical controls, not this eleven-contrast rerun.

### 1.5 Near-copy recall does not rule out reasoning, and a low ceiling does not identify synthesis

**Confirmed inferential overreach.**

Locations: `main.tex:98`–105, 135, 443–457, 846; `figures/fig-ceiling.tex`.

The categorical sentence "A near-ceiling result ... rules out reasoning over ontological structure" is not licensed by this metric. Recovering only exposed entity names does not establish how the model selected the correct names or relations. Likewise, `g = 0` can reflect cancellation between omissions and unexposed recoveries; it is not by itself proof of perfect restatement.

A low ceiling can indicate missing evidence, retrieval failure, vocabulary mismatch, incomplete gold or matcher failure. It does not establish that the supplied evidence is sufficient to synthesize an answer. The paper's own paraphrase experiment is an explicit retrieval-failure counterexample to the proposed low-ceiling diagnosis.

**Required resolution:** frame the control as measuring observable target exposure and delivery, not internal reasoning. State that synthesis requires a separate sufficiency/composition design. Preserve the useful decomposition, but avoid claiming that it establishes absence of reasoning. State explicitly that "ceiling" is a reference baseline, not a mathematical upper bound on all valid answers.

### 1.6 The matcher does not guarantee a lower bound or cancellation of error

**Confirmed limitation of the released matcher.**

Locations: `main.tex:90`, 178, 446–447, 836; `decompose_exposure.py:93`–106.

The matcher can have false positives as well as paraphrase-related false negatives:

- Its exact-title path is an unbounded substring test: a gold title `Rust` matches `trust`; `AI` matches `said`.
- Its long-word threshold ignores ordering, relation roles, negation and whether words occur together.
- Mentioning a correct entity in a negated or wrong relation still counts.

Consequently, absolute recall is not a guaranteed lower bound on semantic correctness. Using one matcher on long structured contexts and shorter answers does not prove first-order error cancellation; the error distributions can differ. Embedding similarity also does not establish entailment or remove these concerns automatically.

**Required resolution:** call this a surface-match proxy; qualify the lower-bound/cancellation claims. Audit false positives and false negatives on an independently annotated sample. If changing token-boundary handling, regenerate all affected results rather than silently replacing the scorer under existing numbers.

### 2.2 The implemented Wilcoxon calculation needs qualification or replacement

Locations: `main.tex:186`, Table `tab:live`; `tools/paper/analyze.py:47`–75.

The script assigns average ranks to tied absolute differences, then uses the **untied normal-approximation variance**. It drops zeros; its comment "Pratt zeros dropped" is incorrect, because Pratt's procedure includes zero differences in ranking before removing their signed contribution. There is no exact small-sample fallback despite the module-level description.

This matters particularly for the general set, which has only two nonzero differences, +1 and +2. The reported normal approximation gives `p=0.179712`; the exact two-sided conditional sign-randomisation test on those ranks gives **p=0.5**.

An independent dynamic-programming enumeration of the sign-randomisation distribution, conditioning on observed tied absolute ranks and dropping zeros, gives:

| Set | Nonzero pairs | Published normal-approximation p | Exact conditional p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| arcane | 13 | 0.033047 | 0.0322265625 |
| thin | 17 | 0.118251 | 0.1304931641 |
| general | 2 | 0.179712 | 0.5 |
| pooled | 32 | 0.003641 | 0.0023037884 |

These are diagnostics, not silently substituted manuscript results. The pooled positive finding remains significant under this check. Choose and document the appropriate test/assumptions, regenerate Holm correction, and update prose, tables and plots together. Mean-difference bootstrap inference and signed-rank inference also target different summaries and should not be presented as identical tests of the same null.

The published rank-biserial effects **do** agree with the actual signed ranks: arcane 0.67033, thin 0.43137, general 1, pooled 0.58902. However, `analyze.py` still emits a sign-count statistic named `cliffs_delta`; it is not standard independent-sample Cliff's delta. Document that the reported rank-biserial values come from the separate decomposition artifact, or consolidate the analysis so rerunning the advertised script reproduces the table's effect-size column.

### 2.3 TOST is asserted but not demonstrated in the released analysis

Locations: `main.tex:186`, 314, 419–440, 818; `figures/fig-forest.tex`.

The paper says it uses TOST with a pre-specified ±0.25 margin, but the inspected statistics script and `analysis.json` provide a two-sided bootstrap percentile interval, not TOST statistics or one-sided p-values. A 95% interval lying wholly inside a margin can support a conservative interval-based equivalence argument, but it should not be labelled a performed conventional TOST without specifying the construction. Conventional TOST at alpha 0.05 is associated with a 90% interval for the corresponding procedure.

**Required resolution:** either implement/report the claimed tests, or describe the actual bootstrap interval-inclusion criterion accurately. Provide a dated record for the pre-specified margin. Acknowledge the discrete, ceiling-heavy general scores: mean loom score is **5.0**, raw **4.95**, and 58/60 pairs tie. This limits the instrument's sensitivity.

The five-model worst-case interval `[-0.63,+0.05]` does not establish equivalence inside ±0.25. Do not generalise the single-model general-set result to that subgroup. Distinguish empirical non-detection of regression from evidence that the gate caused it; there is no matched always-inject ablation here, and Study 2's gate engaged on 78.3% of general questions.

## 3. Interpretation and experimental-design corrections

### 3.1 Non-significance is repeatedly converted into proof of a null or a mechanism

Locations: `main.tex:369`, 452, 846; `figures/fig-forest.tex:39`–44.

The control rerun supports saying that content-specificity is **not established**. It does not show the effect is absent, prove it was underpowered, confirm that any fluent block is equivalent, or "rule out" the stronger claim. In particular, phrases such as "confirmed the effect was underpowered", "an underpowered artefact, not a suppressed signal", and "the fluent-noise arm ... confirms" exceed the reported intervals and correction results.

Likewise, overlapping set-level intervals do not establish a curation-depth interaction. The main text acknowledges this, but the forest caption still says the gradient is not what a prompt-formatting artifact would produce. Soften the caption and any downstream causal attribution, or supply an interaction test/design that separates those effects.

The cited [Power of Noise paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14887v2) studies benefits from adding random documents and discusses possible mechanisms. It is relevant motivation; it does not by itself validate equivalence or establish the precise mechanism of this experiment.

### 3.2 Control definitions and conclusions need to be aligned

Locations: `main.tex:109`–117, 371, 556–600.

- Masking **seed** titles while leaving answer-bearing target names present does not test removal of answer-name matching. The sentence "Survival would indicate name matching rather than knowledge" does not follow from the stated intervention. Explain exactly which competing explanations each arm can and cannot separate.
- Shuffling sentences preserves within-sentence relational facts. Retained performance is not proof of "lexical soup" or lack of use of structure.
- Seed-IRI disjointness does not ensure disjoint gold targets, ancestors, relation endpoints, synonyms or semantic content. Verify target exposure in donor blocks directly before calling a placebo "verified irrelevant". Distinguish this construction check from evidence that the placebo is empirically inert.
- Near-zero gain in irrelevant contexts can simply mean both exposure and recall are near zero; it does not by itself establish content-bound delivery.
- The true control and live loom arm reportedly put context in different message roles: system message in the control (`main.tex:180`) versus the last user message in the node (`main.tex:733`). Identical block bytes alone do not isolate serving-path effects. Report exact message envelopes and budgets.

### 3.3 Composition does not evade the stated copy metric

Locations: abstract; `main.tex:556`–600; `figures/fig-composition.tex`.

The both-premises and b-only arms expose the answer title C verbatim. A copy of the answer-bearing block would therefore score highly under the paper's title-recall metric. The abstract's "where copying cannot help" is misleading: the **both-minus-b-only contrast** is intended to measure the contribution of route information beyond answer exposure. Explain that distinction explicitly.

Other corrections/checks:

- "Every arm serves exactly four ... blocks" excludes the raw arm, which has no context; say every context-bearing arm.
- Saying 99.97% of candidate chains were unusable because the **intermediate class's block** already names the target conflicts with the design, which deliberately needs B's block to name C. The eligibility filter earlier says C must be absent from **A's block**. Verify the subject of the exclusion and supply the original candidate denominator supporting 99.97%.
- Both-minus-b-only is an informative controlled contrast, but adding A also changes relevance cues, attention and retrieval selection; avoid treating it as a pure readout of an internal reasoning process.
- A high b-only baseline limits detectable improvement; it does not prove GPT's null is caused by a ceiling effect. "Consistent with limited headroom" is the supported wording.
- Report all six arms, per-model intervals, model IDs, completion/reasoning budgets, gold construction and significance-family policy. Current composer categories use individual intervals across multiple models; no multiplicity adjustment is disclosed for these categories.
- Masked performance as high as 0.61 is not uniformly "collapse" without corresponding both-premises rates and paired changes.

### 3.5 Model identity and local-model comparisons need stronger provenance

Locations: abstract; `main.tex:178`–184, 556–644.

API display names and model aliases are not immutable versions. Supply exact request/response IDs, run dates, provider route, local checkpoint repository/revision, quantisation, any derivative/fine-tune, server version and inference settings. Existing repository notes mention a Qwen "Heretic" configuration; establish whether it is the measured backend rather than assuming the public family name fully identifies the weights.

The original sweep does not contain Qwen3.8-27B: its Qwen row is **Qwen-2.5-72B**. Ceiling saturation by other models does not establish delivery equivalence for the local 27B. The abstract acknowledges no head-to-head result, but later prose describes Qwen's saturation as measured and the Gemma run as a single-variable replication. ### 3.6 Suppression is observed behaviour, not proof of the proposed mechanism

Location: `main.tex:446`–447.

The raw/grounded comparison on scaffold-unexposed targets is a useful observation, but absence from the scaffold does not make the two knowledge sources "disagree by construction". Omission is not contradiction. Raw correct output also does not prove a fact came exclusively from memorised parameters; lexical clues and inference can contribute.

Use wording such as "recovery without injected context falls after injection"; treat displacement of parametric knowledge as a possible explanation. The 92/760 raw count cannot be independently reconstructed from the available decomposition because original raw sweep rows are absent. Supply the per-question paired flags and inferential analysis if making a strong causal/mechanistic claim.

### 3.7 Reasoning budgets: separate prompt capacity from output allowance

Locations: `main.tex:178`–180, 742–758.

"Generation budgets must cover scaffold-plus-reasoning-plus-answer" conflates the input context window with the completion allowance. Ordinarily the scaffold consumes input/context capacity while reasoning and the final answer consume the configured output allowance; backend-specific semantics should be documented from the actual server. Longer prompts may change how much reasoning is generated without being charged directly against `max_tokens`.

The fixed output budgets demonstrably produced empty answers or truncation. Detecting those is necessary, but always dropping them is not a neutral treatment of deployment failures. Report the operational failure rate and distinguish complete-case answer quality from overall service effectiveness. Temperature zero also does not guarantee deterministic remote inference; reserve "deterministic" for the settings/procedure where justified.

### 3.8 Write-path conclusions outrun the reported methods

Locations: `main.tex:533`–554, 761–787, Appendix A.

For the page-quality result, report the number of unique pages and pairs per arm, how assertions/episodes overlap on a page, aggregation, uncertainty, split sizes and units, the sample behind 93% agreement, and the exact pre-declared decision rule. Repeated pages/episodes are potential dependence clusters.

"The dilution effect ... is the mechanism" is stronger than the experiment identifies; style, placement, length and truth of inserted content are not fully separated. A content/form-controlled study is correctly acknowledged as future work elsewhere.

Blind A/B removes explicit provenance labels; it cannot guarantee the judge cannot infer which text is enriched, or eliminate verbosity/style bias. Cross-family judging limits one concern rather than "avoiding self-preference" categorically.

The lifecycle calls served pages disposable projections rebuilt from the ledger, while mature curated prose is said to pre-exist it and never be edited on landing. Explain how original curated content is retained as a source, or narrow the event-sourcing analogy to the assertion layer. The prose rejects thin-page enrichment as an alternative, but the lifecycle caption retains a conditional thin-page branch; distinguish measured rejection, conditional policy and deployed behaviour.
