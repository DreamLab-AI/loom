When a model answers from a curated corpus via graph retrieval, reported grounding uplift mixes faithful delivery of facts already shown to it with reasoning over injected structure, because gold answers derive from the same corpus. We propose accounting: classify every gold item by whether the shown context exposed it and whether the answer recovered it, and report the four counts beside recall. The ceiling, the recall a verbatim copy of the shown context would achieve, is that table's per-item scalar and the signed over copy its net; both are deterministic and judge-free. On a production node, unaided recall is 0.26 and grounded recall about 0.92, yet across ten models from eight families gain over copy is uniformly negative (-0.067 to -0.022). The accounting is the finding: of 11,360 gold items, three unexposed items are recovered in total, context utilisation spans 0.900–0.955, and models differ only in how much exposed gold they drop. On items the scaffold does not expose, recovery falls from 0.121 bare to 0.004 grounded: injection displaces parametric recall. The uplift is delivery, measured rather than inferred. A paired study on the same node lifts judged quality by +0.27 pooled (p=0.002) and +0.79 where curation is deepest. A uniform-budget control shows the scaffold beats no context but not generic on-corpus text of the same length. Out of domain the general-set interval (+0.05, [0.00,+0.13]) lies inside a pre-specified pm0.25 margin (58/60 pairs tie). On a second domain, typed skill routing over 115 shown rubrics, the scalar moved from +11.6 to +4.7 points under two repairs to one definition of the ceiling while the exposure classification did not move: that is the case for reporting the accounting rather than the scalar. Evaluations whose gold derives from the injected corpus should account for exposure as standard, and report the copy ceiling as its summary.
