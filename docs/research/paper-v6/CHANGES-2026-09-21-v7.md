## New material

### §Routing — "A Second Domain: Typed Skill Routing"
The copy ceiling applied to a discriminative choice task: an agent runtime routes each turn
to one of 115 skills whose rubrics are shown to the judge as options, plus `none`. Gold is
one exposed rubric key, so exposure is total. The copy procedure is adapted to a choice task
as the best judge-free surface match over the exposed options (BM25 and 384-d cosine), each
granted a decline rule whose threshold is oracle-tuned on the evaluated corpus, so the
reported ceiling is flattered and the judge's gain is a floor.

- Judge: `AlexWortega/openjev`, `qwen3.5-4b-nli-v2`, revision pinned; behind the
  Sovereign System One façade (Jev-compatible typed-decision wire protocol) in the
  `agentbox` runtime. Superseded engine (laya, 421M, 256-token shared head budget,
  48-token option cap): 53.5 % top-1, decline collapse — reported as the motivation.
- Corpus: 86 labelled turns, written by the authors; 16 `none`, 70 skill;
  tagged by confusion class and by late-discriminator position (48-token cut).
- Headline (post-adjudication, see below): judge 88.4 % top-1 / 98.8 % top-3. Fair BM25
  ceiling 76.7 / 86.0 (thr 9.1563, 17 declines); fair cosine 67.4 / 76.7 (thr 0.5945, 6
  declines). Gain +11.6 / +12.8 vs BM25; signed per-item +0.116, 95 % CI [+0.012, +0.221],
  16 vs 6, holding from n≈70. Gain +20.9 vs cosine; signed +0.209, [+0.106, +0.312].
- Decomposition (the finding): gain over BM25 concentrates on `none` (+25.0), boundary
  (+33.3), late discriminators (+18.8), single (+20.0); near-neighbour +4.0, early +7.4.
- Label audit and DISCLOSURE: ranker/judge disagreement localises corpus errors. All seven
  disputed turns were re-read against the rubrics' stated boundaries; ONE was corrected
  (turn 14, `browser` → `browser-automation`, on the clause in the `browser` rubric that
  redirects browser tasks), six were left standing against the judge. That single
  correction is what carries the lexical interval clear of zero: pre-adjudication the
  headline was judge 87.2 %, ceiling 77.9 %, signed +0.093, CI [−0.013, +0.199]. The paper
  reports both figures and names the pre-adjudication ones as conservative. The audit was
  run before the headline was fixed, the criterion was the rubric's text rather than any
  model's answer, and the two plain judge errors among the seven were left in place.
- Engine-side tables (explicitly scoped as not bearing on the ceiling): decline-threshold
  sweep (plateau 0.25–0.40 at 89.5 %; the deployed 0.5 used throughout the section sits
  just outside it and costs 1.1 points, which is the conservative direction), escalation
  cascade (40 % escalation lands within one turn of the judge for two-fifths of the
  latency; the curve is not monotone at n=86; oracle union 95.3 %, agreement 74.4 %),
  confidence calibration (AUC 0.728; max confidence on a wrong answer 0.548;
  systematically under-confident). All three re-measured on the corrected corpus — the
  sweep as 9 fresh full passes (774 inferences), the other two recomputed from per-turn
  records.
- Reproduction: `system-one-eval run --concurrency 1 --timeout-ms 120000` (the engine is
  serialised by a tokeniser lock; the default concurrency queues past the timeout), then
  `system-one-eval copy-ceiling --from-report … --ceilings both`. Embeddings are requested
  one text per call: the estate's embedding endpoint returns global batch offsets in
  `data[].index` when concurrent requests merge (verified 2026-09-20).

### §Ideal State — "A Private Corporate Data Lake"
Seven properties (D1–D7) of a private data lake that takes the paper's findings seriously,
each marked *measured* or *specified*, organised around one stated through-line: the same
judge-free control at every seam, so each decision about private data is measured against
a verbatim copy of its own input before it is trusted. Justified from the ten-model result,
the promotion-gate refusal and the routing replication. Names the served node's own
promotion and judged-page gates as the next seam to move behind the typed-decision façade.

## Not done / caveats
- **The cloud judge (TypeSafe Jev) was not run on this corpus**: across six full attempts
  the endpoint answered 0 of 86 turns, returning 503 "no healthy upstream", 529
  `system_overloaded` and 403. Our client was eliminated as the cause (correct bearer and
  User-Agent, same header shape as the working production hook, same result on a 300-byte
  and a 60 KB body; one isolated probe did succeed). The v6 text nowhere compares the 4B judge against a cloud number, and the ADR
  baseline the runtime cites (90 % soft) was measured on a different case set and is not
  comparable; if a Jev run on these 86 turns completes it belongs in `tab:routing` as a
  column and under the same ceiling. A single successful probe returned
  `browser-automation` at 0.98 for the `browser` example — the same answer as the 4B judge,
  and the same disagreement with the corpus label.
- The routing corpus is self-authored and single-seed; the paper says so in three places,
  and the re-adjudication disclosure above is the fourth.
- The `none` threshold sweep (`tab:routing-sweep`) was re-run on the corrected corpus after
  the relabel, as were the cascade and calibration tables.
- Nothing in this revision has been peer reviewed; it is an arXiv replacement.
